ANDROID

See which apps on your Android phone are tracking you

WhoSees shows, live on a map, which app on your phone is sending data, which company receives it, and where in the world it goes. Free, open source, no root and no account.

Download for Android ↓

What you'll see

  • Which app is sending data, by its real name.
  • Which company receives it: Google, Meta, Amazon, or an advertising or analytics firm you've never heard of. Trackers and advertisers are labelled.
  • Where in the world it goes, drawn live on a map from wherever you are.
  • What kind of data it probably is, like your location or advertising ID, plus a privacy report for each app.
  • Warnings when an app connects to a known scam website or malware server.

How to install WhoSees on Android

  1. On your phone, tap Download for Android above to get WhoSees-Android.apk (about 80 MB).
  2. Open the downloaded file. If Android says your browser isn't allowed to install apps, tap Settings, turn on Allow from this source, then go back.
  3. Tap Install. Because WhoSees isn't on Google Play yet, Google Play Protect may ask you to scan the app or confirm. Choose to install it.
  4. Open WhoSees, set your city if asked, and tap Start live view.

Why Android asks to "set up a VPN connection"

The first time you press Start, Android asks whether WhoSees may set up a VPN connection. Tap OK. A VPN is the only way Android lets an app see where other apps send data.

IT STAYS ON YOUR PHONE

WhoSees isn't a VPN service. Your traffic isn't sent to any server: WhoSees notes where each connection goes and passes it straight on, so your apps work exactly as before. You'll see a key icon while it runs, and it stops the moment you press Stop.

Private DNS and website names

If your phone uses Private DNS with a named provider, the names of the websites your apps visit are encrypted, so WhoSees can't show them. You'll still see which app, which company and which country. WhoSees tells you when this is the case. Read about Private DNS.

Updating and uninstalling

To update, download the newest version from this page and install it over the old one. Your settings are kept. To remove WhoSees: Settings → Apps → WhoSees → Uninstall.

Questions

Does WhoSees need root?

No. WhoSees uses Android's built-in VPN feature, which any app can use with your permission. No root or special setup is needed.

Is WhoSees a VPN service?

No. Android calls it a VPN because that's the only way an app can see where other apps send data. Everything stays on your phone: nothing is sent to a VPN server or to us, and your data goes exactly where it was going anyway.

Will it drain my battery?

Only while it's running. WhoSees runs only while you have a live view on, and stops completely when you press Stop.

Is WhoSees on Google Play?

Not yet. For now, install it from this page. A Google Play version is planned.
